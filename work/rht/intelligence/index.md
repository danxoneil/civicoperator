## The opposite of a firehose

Anyone can scrape fifty state websites. The result is noise you still have to read, dedupe, and vet.

Raw trackers push links at you all day — most of them irrelevant to what you sell, none of them vetted. You still have to open each one and decide whether it's real and whether it fits. That's analyst time you don't have.

A one-time brief has the opposite problem. It's the right way to find your fit, but the program keeps moving. Solicitations, awards, and bills land every week, and a snapshot goes stale the quarter after you buy it.

This is neither. It's the things that fit *your* organization, read by a person before they reach you, with the deadlines and contacts the state has published. Judgment, not a dashboard.

## One program, all the way down

Civic Operator runs a single instrument pointed at a single program. The Rural Health Transformation Program, the fifty state programs underneath it, the agencies administering them, the organizations winning the money, and the vendors selling into those organizations. Solicitations, awards, legislation, budget movements, governance changes, named people. It has been fed every day since the program started, and it doesn't do anything else.

That depth is the whole asset. A general-purpose tracker knows a little about a lot of programs. This one knows a single program the way you know your own product.

## What you actually get

A digest, by email, most business days. Not an alert per item — one piece of writing you can read in a few minutes and work from.

Solicitations and awards are the spine of it. Legislation, state plans, and changes in who is running a program come with them as they surface.

Every item tells you what it is, why it matched you, and where to read the source. Most carry considerably more, wherever the public record supports it: when it's due, where it sits in that state's timeline, which organizations are already working the topic, and who to call — by name, with title, and with the email and phone number that have been published.

Contacts aren't a separate product and they aren't rationed. Knowing who to call is most of what this is for.

## Nothing reaches you unread

The machine finds. The operator vouches.

Matching runs automatically across what fifty states publish. Then a person reads what it surfaced — me, or someone working under my direction — and decides what's worth your attention. You don't get "probably relevant." You get the things I'd walk down the hall to tell you about, which is the arrangement this is meant to approximate.

That judgment is the part that can't be automated, and it's the part you're paying for.

What it isn't is a promise that a state won't move a deadline the week after I've read the page. Public procurement changes without notice. The source link is on every item so you can confirm it before you commit to anything.

## It takes the shape you need

Nobody buys this the same way.

Some clients want the daily read and nothing else. Some want everything moving in three states and don't care about the other forty-seven. Some want the awardee side — who has won money in a state, and the named people at those organizations who buy what they sell, arriving with the items they relate to. Some want the rubric retuned every few weeks as their strategy moves, and some set it once and leave it.

All of that is the same instrument, pointed differently. We work out the shape in the first month and change it whenever it stops fitting. There's no tier you have to buy your way into and no configuration you're locked out of.

## And it stays yours

Your criteria, your record.

You direct the rubric — your states, your topics, your read on what a good fit looks like. Ordinary tuning is part of the engagement, not a change order. As your strategy moves, the matching moves with it.

Everything you've been sent stays on a running record, kept next to the rubric that produced it, so the pipeline you've been building doesn't disappear into old email. You can have it as an export whenever you want it, and it goes with you if you go.
