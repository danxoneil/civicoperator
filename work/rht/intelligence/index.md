## The opposite of a firehose

Anyone can scrape fifty state websites. The result is noise you still have to read, dedupe, and vet.

### The firehose problem

Raw trackers push dozens of links a day — most irrelevant to you, none vetted. You still have to open each one and decide whether it's real and whether it fits. That's analyst time you don't have.

### The point-in-time problem

A one-time brief is the right way to find your fit — but the program moves. New NOFOs, RFPs, awards, and bills land every week, and a snapshot goes stale the fiscal quarter after you buy it.

### What this is instead

The two or three things that fit *your* organization, checked by a human before they reach you, with the contacts and deadlines the state has published. Judgment, not a dashboard.

## What lands in your inbox

A single, scannable digest — not per-alert spam. Every opportunity carries the first two. The rest come with it whenever the state has published them.

01

### What it is

The procurement, award, or legislative action, in plain language, with a link to the source document.

02

### Why it fits you

The specific funded priorities and dollars in your rubric it maps to — so you see the fit, not just the item.

03

### The clock

Published deadlines and where the item sits in the state's timeline, so you know what's urgent and what can wait.

04

### Who to call

The state contacts, subrecipients, awardees, and other organizations already active on that topic, where the state has published them — so you're not starting from a blank page.

A digest most mornings — never fewer than twelve a month, and in practice a good deal more. When something genuinely can't wait, I'll text you.

## What "verified" means

This is the difference between an alert service and an intelligence service.

### A human checks it before you see it

Matching runs automatically across every state's output — but nothing reaches you until I've read it and stand behind it. You don't get "probably relevant." You get opportunities that have been checked against your rubric by a person. That verification step is the entire product: it's why a paying client can act on the digest without re-doing the work.
