## Questions & answers

What is New Mexico's Rural Health Transformation Program allocation, and how is the funding structured?

New Mexico was awarded **$211,484,741** for Federal Fiscal Year 2026 (FFY26) through the CMS Rural Health Transformation Program — its share of the federal $50 billion program, and a figure the state confirmed in its Administrative Services Organization RFP. It is a five-year award (roughly $1 billion over the grant period), structured with Year-1 funding released through competitive procurements and subawards, subject to annual CMS approval and a program-wide administrative-cost cap of 10%.

Which agency administers New Mexico's RHTP, and who leads it?

The [New Mexico Health Care Authority (HCA)](https://www.hca.nm.gov/rural-health-transformation-program/) administers the program. HCA is running a deliberately lean internal team — hiring roughly 17 full-time staff plus a wave of program roles (Finance Director, Operations Manager, Data & Evaluation Manager, and initiative managers) — and leaning on outsourced vendors as the operational engines. Federal program inquiries route to [MAHARural@cms.hhs.gov](mailto:MAHARural@cms.hhs.gov).

What is New Mexico's rural health transformation plan trying to accomplish?

New Mexico is implementing five approved RHT initiatives focused on expanding access, strengthening rural health infrastructure, closing maternal-care deserts, and advancing health equity across rural, frontier and Tribal communities. A defining feature is durability: HCA is building a permanent **Center for Rural Health Sustainability & Innovation (CRHSI/RHSIC)** embedded in the agency and designed to outlive the five-year grant via a revenue-sharing model. New Mexico plans to push roughly 95% of its federal RHTP funding out the door through competitive procurements and direct subawards.

[New Mexico RHTP hub →](https://www.hca.nm.gov/rural-health-transformation-program/)

Where is the money going — which initiatives is New Mexico funding, and at what share?

- **Rural Health Innovation Fund (RHIF)** — the primary community-led micro-grant vehicle, ~$187.5M over the grant ($47M in Year 1), for locally designed projects spanning behavioral health, preventive care, equipment/facility upgrades and care-delivery technology.
- **Healthy Horizons** — the specialty-care and chronic-disease initiative (~$393.2M), anchored by Regional Hub Organizations, plus a carve-out statewide maternal health allocation.
- **Administrative Services Organization (ASO)** — the fiscal back-office managing intake, subrecipient scopes, invoicing and compliance.
- **CRHSI / Rooted in New Mexico** — shared technical assistance and community programming.

Key project tags include Health Equity, Infrastructure and Maternal Deserts. The full breakdown is in New Mexico's budget narrative.

What has New Mexico procured so far, and what's open right now?

New Mexico has posted several major procurements. The **ASO RFP (#26-630-1000-0019)**, released April 30, 2026, seeks the fiscal anchor across four service domains (the federal RHTP is Domain 3); its deadline was extended to June 5, 2026 with award anticipated July 10. The **CRHSI RFP** closed June 12, 2026. The **Rural Health Innovation Fund RFP (#27-630-1000-0004)** made ~$47M available, with proposals due July 27, 2026 via Submittable and awards anticipated October 1, 2026 — notably, cost is not scored. The **Healthy Horizons RFA** for Regional Hubs was extended to July 6, 2026, and an **On-site Clinic RFP (#27-630-0700-0004)** with an optional rural mobile component was due August 1, 2026. The running record is in the dispatches below.

Recent key dates: RHIF proposals due **July 27, 2026** (evaluation July 28–August 17, finalist selection ~August 14, best-and-final offers August 20, awards as soon as October 1, 2026); On-site Clinic proposals due **August 1, 2026**; Healthy Horizons applications due July 6 with contracts starting September 1. As those windows close, new deadlines and webinars surface in the dispatches below and on HCA's open-RFP portal.

What legislation or state action enables New Mexico's RHTP?

The Rural Health Transformation Program was created by **Section 71401 of the One Big Beautiful Bill Act (2025)** and is administered federally by CMS. New Mexico administers its award through the Health Care Authority. State law shapes how bids are scored: under **Section 13-1-21(J) NMSA 1978**, local (Resident, Veteran and Native American) preference points cannot be applied to federally funded work, so bidders on the federal RHTP domain compete strictly on merit and cost. The ASO also folds in state-funded streams including Senate Bill 3 behavioral health reform.

How can vendors, providers, or consultants engage with New Mexico's program?

Eligibility runs on two tracks. **Direct-to-state contracts** go out as competitive RFPs where firms bid as **prime contractors** — the ASO, CRHSI and On-site Clinic procurements are examples (note Guidehouse, which wrote New Mexico's CMS application, is not precluded from bidding on implementation work). **Subgrants and subawards** flow to rural providers, Tribal entities, local public health, community-based organizations, local governments and academic institutions — and RHIF explicitly welcomes vendors, technology partners and equipment suppliers that can show a direct implementation role. This is where consultants most often engage as **subcontractors or implementation partners**.
