## Questions & answers

What is Missouri's Rural Health Transformation Program allocation, and how is the funding structured?

Missouri's Year 1 award is **approximately $215.9 million**, its share of the federal $50 billion Rural Health Transformation Program, structured by CMS as a five-year program. The state has branded its plan **ToRCH Care** (Transformation of Rural Community Health Care).

Which agency administers Missouri's RHTP, and who leads it?

The [Missouri Department of Social Services (DSS)](https://mydss.mo.gov/mhd/rural-health), through its MO HealthNet Division and Rural Health Transformation Office, administers the program on behalf of CMS. It is led by RHT Director **Joseph Miller**, coordinating with the Department of Mental Health (DMH) and the Department of Health and Senior Services (DHSS). Program questions go to [ruralhealth@health.mo.gov](mailto:ruralhealth@health.mo.gov).

What is Missouri's rural health transformation plan trying to accomplish?

Missouri's ToRCH Care model organizes the state into **27 Community Hub Areas**, each led by a competitively selected Hub Anchor organization that convenes local providers and community partners. Underpinning it is a **Digital Backbone** — a foundational technology and data layer (the Missouri Integrated Health Data Fabric, or MOFAB) built on HL7 FHIR R4 and USCDI v3 standards — alongside innovative payment models, talent-pipeline development, and regional networks.

[Missouri ToRCH Care hub →](https://mydss.mo.gov/mhd/rural-health)

Where is the money going — which initiatives is Missouri funding, and at what share?

- **Digital Backbone** — roughly $56 million in Year 1, covering EHR modernization, a statewide Social Care Referral platform, ambient AI, remote patient monitoring, telehealth, and data/AI governance.
- **27 Hub Anchors** — one organization per ToRCH Care Community Hub Area.
- **MEMSA Mobile Integrated Healthcare / Community Paramedicine** — roughly $6.5 million.
- **Missouri Doula Association** — $732,660.43 for rural doula training and integration.
- **Rural home visiting** (via the Children's Trust Fund) — $588,000 annually.

The full breakdown, including talent pipeline, EHR upgrades, and regional networks, is in Missouri's budget narrative.

What has Missouri procured so far, and what's open right now?

Missouri is taking a distinct structural path: rather than run open state RFPs, DSS largely routes RHTP dollars through **named administering intermediaries**. It selected 27 Hub Anchor organizations (from 41 applications) through a competitive review; partnered with the **Missouri Emergency Medical Services Association (MEMSA)** on a roughly $6.5M Mobile Integrated Healthcare build (MEMSA is now recruiting rural EMS agencies directly and this is open now); awarded the **Missouri Doula Association** $732,660.43; and opened **Rural Health Transformation Funding for Home Visiting** through the Children's Trust Fund. The dispatch list below carries the running record.

- **August 23, 2026** — Rural Health Transformation Funding for Home Visiting application period closes (via the Children's Trust Fund).
- **Open now** — MEMSA's Mobile Integrated Healthcare / Community Paramedicine recruitment for rural EMS agencies (funded through July 31, 2027; questions to RHTP@memsa.org or 573-370-1702).
- **July 18, 2026** — 27 Hub Anchor awardees announced.

New dates surface first in the dated dispatches below.

What legislation or state action enables Missouri's RHTP?

The Rural Health Transformation Program was created by **Section 71401 of the One Big Beautiful Bill Act (2025)** and is administered federally by CMS. In Missouri, the Department of Social Services (MO HealthNet Division) administers the award on behalf of CMS, coordinating with the Department of Mental Health and the Department of Health and Senior Services. Detailed allocation figures are set out in Missouri's ToRCH Care project and budget narratives.

How can vendors, providers, or consultants engage with Missouri's program?

Eligibility runs on two tracks. **Direct-to-state contracts** can go out as competitive solicitations where firms bid as **prime contractors** — but Missouri leans heavily on **designated intermediaries** instead: funds flow through MEMSA (for rural EMS), the Missouri Doula Association, the Children's Trust Fund (for home visiting), and the 27 Hub Anchors that lead each Community Hub Area. Rural providers — hospitals, FQHCs, behavioral-health organizations, local public health agencies, EMS agencies, and universities — engage as **subawardees and applicants** to these intermediaries, and technology vendors and consultants most often engage as **subcontractors or implementation partners** to Hub Anchors and the Digital Backbone build.

Missouri does not run a single central RFP portal; watch the [ToRCH Care hub](https://mydss.mo.gov/mhd/rural-health) and DSS announcements, and direct questions to [ruralhealth@health.mo.gov](mailto:ruralhealth@health.mo.gov).
