## Questions & answers

What is Oregon's Rural Health Transformation Program allocation, and how is the funding structured?

Oregon's Year 1 award is **approximately $197.3 million** as its share of the federal $50 billion Rural Health Transformation Program, structured by CMS as a five-year program. CMS lifted restrictions on all Oregon RHTP funds on March 20, 2026 (previously only state staffing dollars were accessible), clearing the roughly $197M intended for subawardees to move; Oregon has since pushed more than $175 million out the door in 2026.

Which agency administers Oregon's RHTP, and who leads it?

The [Oregon Health Authority (OHA)](https://www.oregon.gov/oha/hpa/hp/pages/rural-health-transformation.aspx) administers the program through its Health Policy & Analytics division, with the **Oregon Office of Rural Health (ORH)** convening partners and running vendor engagement. Program questions go to [RHTP@oha.oregon.gov](mailto:RHTP@oha.oregon.gov).

What is Oregon's rural health transformation plan trying to accomplish?

Oregon is moving money fast to hit the Year 1 metrics CMS will use to judge future funding, deploying dollars through a mix of one competitive round (the Catalyst Awards) and several non-competitive channels — direct Transformation Funds to rural providers, a Tribal Initiative set-aside, and OHA-selected Immediate Impact grants. Priorities span maternal and newborn care, workforce equity and pipeline development, behavioral health and substance use, chronic disease, remote monitoring, and cybersecurity.

[Oregon RHTP hub →](https://www.oregon.gov/oha/hpa/hp/pages/rural-health-transformation.aspx)

Where is the money going — which initiatives is Oregon funding, and at what share?

By mid-2026 Oregon had awarded roughly **$175.3 million**, most of it outside competitive procurement:

- **Catalyst Awards** — $80.1M in Budget Period 1 ($156.2M across two periods) to 85 organizations running 103 projects (the one competitive round).
- **Immediate Impact Awards** — $6.5M to 12 projects (April) plus $17M to 33 projects, non-competitive and OHA-selected.
- **Tribal Initiative set-aside** — $21.7M for the Nine Federally Recognized Tribes of Oregon.
- **Transformation Funds** (direct allocations) — $35M to rural hospitals, $10M to certified rural health clinics, $5M to local public health authorities.

What has Oregon procured so far, and what's open right now?

Oregon's one competitive round, the **Catalyst Awards RFGP** (posted on OregonBuys), closed and named winners July 8, 2026: 85 organizations, 103 projects, $156.2M across two budget periods. The strategic tell is that most of Oregon's newest money — the Transformation Funds, the Tribal set-aside, and two waves of Immediate Impact grants, roughly $95 million — went out **without a public RFP** an outside vendor could bid into. For vendors and consultants, the live opening is downstream: ORH is funding regional convenings (invitations available on request) to build the partnerships behind regionally identified applications by the end of Year 2, and is planning a vendor roadshow and an October vendor fair. The dispatch list below carries the running record.

What are the key upcoming dates and deadlines in Oregon?

- **End of August 2026** — Oregon's Year 1 federal progress report is due to CMS.
- **July 8, 2026** — Catalyst Award winners announced.
- **October 2026** — ORH's expanded rural health conference includes an RHTP vendor fair.
- **End of Year 2** — target for regionally identified RHTP grant applications.

With Catalyst closed, most remaining pathways are non-competitive; new dates surface first in the dated dispatches below.

What legislation or state action enables Oregon's RHTP?

The Rural Health Transformation Program was created by **Section 71401 of the One Big Beautiful Bill Act (2025)** and is administered federally by CMS. In Oregon, CMS lifted restrictions on all RHTP funds on March 20, 2026 — previously only state staffing dollars were accessible while the roughly $197M for subawardees was frozen — which cleared the Catalyst Awards RFGP and the direct funding pathways to move. Detailed allocation figures are set out in Oregon's project and budget narratives.

How can vendors, providers, or consultants engage with Oregon's program?

Eligibility runs on two tracks. **Direct-to-state contracts** go out as competitive solicitations — principally the Catalyst Awards RFGP — where firms bid as **prime contractors**. But much of Oregon's money flows **non-competitively**: Transformation Funds go straight to rural hospitals, certified rural health clinics, and local public health authorities; a Tribal set-aside goes to the Nine Federally Recognized Tribes; and Immediate Impact grants are OHA-selected. Vendors and consultants most often engage as **subcontractors or implementation partners** to those subawardees rather than as direct grantees.

Complete OHA's [Vendor Interest Form](https://app.smartsheet.com/b/form/019c8c4dc64574cc8d9c0507aaba0e98) to be listed in the public directory, request an ORH regional convening invitation, and watch the [Catalyst Awards RFGP on OregonBuys](https://oregonbuys.gov/bso/external/bidDetail.sdo?docId=S-44300-00001537&external=true&parentUrl=close) and the [RHTP hub](https://www.oregon.gov/oha/hpa/hp/pages/rural-health-transformation.aspx).
