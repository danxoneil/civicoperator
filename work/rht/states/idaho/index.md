## Questions & answers

What is Idaho's Rural Health Transformation Program allocation, and how is the funding structured?

Idaho's total award is **$185,974,367.81**, its share of the federal $50 billion Rural Health Transformation Program under CMS federal award RHTCMS332084 (ALN 93.798). The program is a five-year CMS cooperative agreement. Idaho's published Year 1 milestone is a hard one: **all Year 1 funds must be obligated by October 30, 2026** — a deadline that is now visibly shaping how the state designs, times and routes its procurements.

Which agency administers Idaho's RHTP, and who leads it?

The [Idaho Department of Health and Welfare (DHW)](https://healthandwelfare.idaho.gov/providers/rural-health-transformation-program-grant/about-rural-health-transformation-program-grant) administers the program, with questions routed to [IdahoRHTP@dhw.idaho.gov](mailto:IdahoRHTP@dhw.idaho.gov). Specific pieces run through DHW divisions — for example, the Division of Behavioral Health leads the Pediatric Psychiatry Access Line procurement. A legislative RHT Oversight Committee reviews the program, and DHW holds regular lobbyist and stakeholder calls.

What is Idaho's rural health transformation plan trying to accomplish?

Idaho organizes its plan around five initiatives: (1) improving rural access to care through technology — facility technology assessments, telehealth, digital health, cybersecurity and AI tools, emergency communications, data analytics and EHR upgrades; (2) innovative care-delivery models — diagnostic and care-access innovations, health extenders, and EMS expansion; (3) sustaining the rural workforce through ladder payments, career pathways, work-based learning and graduate medical education; (4) population-specific, evidence-based projects in chronic disease, behavioral health, and maternal and child health; and (5) rural health infrastructure and partnerships — facility renovations, pharmacy solutions, clinical equipment, patient-transport vehicles, safety-code compliance and Tribal support. The five initiatives contemplate roughly 23 targeted procurements.

Where is the money going — which initiatives is Idaho funding, and at what share?

The single largest Year 1 vehicle is an **$83,700,800 competitive subgrant pool** for technology, equipment, renovation and vehicle enhancements — telehealth pods, diagnostic and telepharmacy kiosks, AI-enabled cybersecurity, clinical equipment, mobile health units, patient-transport vehicles and targeted renovations — open to rural health clinics, FQHCs, hospitals, Tribes, long-term care and local public health districts. Direct-to-state RFPs in the pipeline include Data Analytics & Outcomes Evaluation (~$2M), Project Management Support (~$1.3M), Facility Renovation Independent Verification (~$2M), a Facility/Technology Assessment (~$2.2M), workforce/care-gap needs assessments (~$2.79M), and a Ladder Payments third-party administrator (~$800K overseeing $13.72M in Year 1 incentive payments). Workforce Residency, Broadband and Pharmacy Support are core project tags; the full breakdown is in Idaho's procurement overview and budget narrative. Federal caps apply: infrastructure ≤20%, EHR replacement ≤5%, administrative ≤10%.

What has Idaho procured so far, and what's open right now?

Idaho has posted a steady stream of solicitations. Live and recent items include the **Technology Assessment RFP (RHRFP2606 / IPRO RFP1375)** — posted July 10, proposals due August 21 — whose winner effectively drafts the requirements for downstream technology procurements; the **Pediatric Psychiatry Access Line (RHTPPAL1)**; two Maternal & Child Health subawards (OB Readiness RHTPMCH1 and Perinatal Quality Collaborative RHTPMCH2); a Ladder Payments TPA RFP; and Facility Renovation Independent Verification. Notably, Idaho has routed several connective-tissue contracts through **cooperative-contract vehicles rather than open bid** — Project Management Support (~$1.3M), a Rural Cybersecurity Modernization Assessment, and, after canceling its open RFP on July 13, the Data Analytics & Outcomes Evaluation contract (~$2M). What are the key upcoming dates and deadlines in Idaho?

Key recent dates: the Technology Assessment RFP held its pre-proposal conference July 17, with questions due July 24, **proposals due August 21 (5:00 p.m. MT)** and public opening August 24; its anticipated contract term runs October 1, 2026 through September 30, 2028, extendable to five years. The Pediatric Psychiatry Access Line held a pre-application webinar August 4, and the Maternal & Child Health subawards (OB Readiness, Perinatal Quality Collaborative) closed July 10. Everything is pinned to the **October 30, 2026** Year 1 obligation deadline. Live windows surface first in the dated dispatches below and on the funding page.

What legislation or state action enables Idaho's RHTP?

The Rural Health Transformation Program was created by **Section 71401 of the One Big Beautiful Bill Act (2025)** and is administered federally by CMS. In Idaho, the award is administered through DHW under CMS federal award RHTCMS332084, with legislative review by an RHT Oversight Committee. State law shapes specific procurements: Idaho's interagency-contracting authority under **Idaho Code §67-2332A** is what limits the Pediatric Psychiatry Access Line to the state's public universities, and Idaho procurement rules underpin its use of cooperative-contract vehicles for several infrastructure contracts.

How can vendors, providers, or consultants engage with Idaho's program?

Eligibility runs on two tracks, and Idaho splits them explicitly. **Direct-to-state contracts** go out as competitive RFPs (or cooperative contracts), where firms bid as **prime contractors** — project management, data/evaluation, technology and facility assessments, cybersecurity and the Ladder Payments administrator; some are narrowed to Idaho universities or public entities by statute. **Subgrants and subawards** flow to rural providers — critical access hospitals, rural health clinics, the state's rural emergency hospital, FQHCs, Tribal health organizations, pharmacies, EMS agencies and long-term care — and vendors of telehealth pods, diagnostic kiosks and analytics tools should market to these funded recipients as **subcontractors or implementation partners**. Bidders route through Idaho's Infor/LUMA state supplier portal.
