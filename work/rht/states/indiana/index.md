## Questions & answers

What is Indiana's Rural Health Transformation Program allocation, and how is the funding structured?

Indiana received **approximately $206.9 million** in Year 1 funding under the federal $50 billion Rural Health Transformation Program, part of the CMS awards announced December 29, 2025. As with every state, the award is disbursed by CMS annually over five years, with each year's payment tied to performance.

Which agency administers Indiana's RHTP, and who leads it?

The [Indiana Department of Health (IDOH)](https://www.in.gov/grow-rural-health/) owns the program, branded **GROW: Cultivating Hoosier Health**. Competitive procurements are run for IDOH by the Indiana Department of Administration (IDOA), with IDOA's Mike Huth serving as buyer on several RHTP RFPs. Program contact: [lbouza@health.in.gov](mailto:lbouza@health.in.gov).

What is Indiana's rural health transformation plan trying to accomplish?

GROW: Cultivating Hoosier Health brings the federal program to the state with a locally driven design — Indiana's premise is that rural communities know their own needs best, and it has stood up regional committees with dozens of members to shape local solutions. Early initiatives span a statewide Medical Operations Coordinator Center for transfer and EMS coordination, alternate Medicaid payment models (including a rural ACO), a rural clinical-training and preceptor pipeline, and a telehealth landscape assessment. Tracked priorities include cardiac access, pediatric telehealth, and a rural connectivity build-out.

[Indiana GROW hub →](https://www.in.gov/grow-rural-health/)

Where is the money going — which initiatives is Indiana funding, and at what share?

Indiana's spending picture is emerging through its numbered RHTP initiatives rather than published percentage shares. Visible initiatives include Initiative 1 (Medical Operations Coordinator Center), Initiative 7 (Teleconsult/Telehealth Landscape Assessment), and Initiative 10 (Clinical Training and Readiness — rural physician and preceptor stipends plus a centralized preceptor registry), alongside an ACO feasibility study and a program-evaluator contract. Tracked project tags are cardiac access, pediatric telehealth, and rural connectivity. The full breakdown is set out in Indiana's RHTP application and budget narrative to CMS.

What has Indiana procured so far, and what's open right now?

Indiana issued a cluster of RHTP RFPs through the IDOA bids portal: RFP 26-87448 (MOCC Operations, a 5-year contract, due June 15); RFP 26-87556 (Clinical Training and Readiness / preceptor registry, Initiative 10, due June 15); RFP 26-87667 (ACO Feasibility Study, with a June 11 pre-proposal conference); RHTP07 (Teleconsult/Telehealth Landscape Assessment, due May 18); and an RHTP Admin Program Evaluator (due May 18). Watch the broad IDOA Current Business Opportunities portal, not just the regional grants page — that is where these post. The dispatch list below carries the running record.

Indiana's early RFP deadlines (May 18 and June 15, 2026) and pre-proposal conferences (May 20 and June 11) are now past. Additional solicitations post to the [IDOA Current Business Opportunities portal](https://www.in.gov/idoa/procurement/current-business-opportunities/) on a rolling basis; live dates surface first in the dated dispatches below. Subscribe to Indiana's RHTP updates to be notified as new opportunities open.

What legislation or state action enables Indiana's RHTP?

The Rural Health Transformation Program was created by **Section 71401 of the One Big Beautiful Bill Act (2025)** and is administered federally by CMS. Indiana administers its award through IDOH under its CMS-approved application and budget, with procurement run through IDOA; no separate enabling state statute is named in the record. Note a key structural rule Indiana added to its FAQ: primary subrecipients are prohibited from awarding funds to other subrecipients via sub-awards or sub-grants, though they may still engage contractors.

How can vendors, providers, or consultants engage with Indiana's program?

Eligibility runs on two tracks. **Direct-to-state contracts** go out as competitive IDOA RFPs, where firms bid as **prime contractors** — the route for most of Indiana's early administrative and infrastructure work (MOCC, evaluator, telehealth assessment, preceptor registry). **Subgrants and subawards** flow to rural providers — hospitals, Rural Health Clinics, FQHCs, EMS agencies and community organizations — where technology vendors and consultants most often engage as **subcontractors or implementation partners**. Note Indiana bars subrecipients from re-granting funds onward, so partners engage as contractors, not sub-subgrantees.

Indiana maintains a self-service vendor intake list (add your firm via the state's form) and publishes it weekly. Track opportunities on the IDOA portal and subscribe for GROW updates.
