## Questions & answers

What is Hawaii's Rural Health Transformation Program allocation, and how is the funding structured?

Hawaii's award is **approximately $188.9 million** in Year-1 funding, its share of the federal $50 billion Rural Health Transformation Program. CMS structures the program over five years, with a Year-1 allocation followed by additional annual funding across the period. Hawaii's detailed Year-1 versus five-year budget structure is set out in the state's CMS-approved application and budget narrative.

Which agency administers Hawaii's RHTP, and who leads it?

Hawaii's program is administered by the **Hawaii Department of Health**, through its Office of Primary Care and Rural Health. Program contact is [doh.opcpdh@doh.hawaii.gov](mailto:doh.opcpdh@doh.hawaii.gov), and program information is published on the state's engagement hub.

[Hawaii RHTP hub →](https://engage.hawaii.gov/rhtp/)

What is Hawaii's rural health transformation plan trying to accomplish?

Based on the state's published focus areas, Hawaii's plan centers on **Native Hawaiian health hubs** and **tele-robotic surgery** — signaling an emphasis on culturally grounded rural care delivery and on extending specialty surgical capacity to remote and island communities. Fuller detail on the plan's priorities and initiatives is published on the state's RHTP hub.

[Hawaii RHTP hub →](https://engage.hawaii.gov/rhtp/)

Where is the money going — which initiatives is Hawaii funding, and at what share?

The focus areas on record for Hawaii are **Native Hawaiian health hubs** and **tele-robotic surgery**. The state has not yet published a detailed public initiative-by-initiative budget breakdown in the primary sources reviewed; the full allocation is set out in Hawaii's CMS-approved budget narrative, and specific spending categories will surface as the state posts them to its hub.

What legislation or state action enables Hawaii's RHTP?

The Rural Health Transformation Program was created by **Section 71401 of the One Big Beautiful Bill Act (2025)** and is administered federally by CMS. Hawaii administers its award through the Hawaii Department of Health under its CMS-approved application. No additional Hawaii-specific enabling statute is named in the primary sources reviewed.

How can vendors, providers, or consultants engage with Hawaii's program?

Eligibility is expected to run on two tracks. **Direct-to-state contracts** go out as competitive RFPs, where firms bid as **prime contractors**. **Subgrants and subawards** flow to rural providers — hospitals, Rural Health Clinics, FQHCs, Native Hawaiian and Tribal health organizations, EMS agencies, and counties — and it is here that technology vendors and consultants most often engage as **subcontractors or implementation partners**. Until Hawaii posts its first solicitations, the best move is to monitor the state's engagement hub and reach the Department of Health's Office of Primary Care and Rural Health directly.
