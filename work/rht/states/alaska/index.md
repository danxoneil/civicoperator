## Questions & answers

What is Alaska's Rural Health Transformation Program allocation, and how is the funding structured?

Alaska's Budget Period 1 award is approximately **$272.2 million**, its share of the federal $50 billion Rural Health Transformation Program and among the largest per-capita awards in the country. It is a five-year program; the state opened its funding pipeline with a Letter-of-Interest round rather than immediate RFPs, and demand vastly outstripped supply — roughly $2.5 billion in year-one asks were submitted against the available funding.

Which agency administers Alaska's RHTP, and who leads it?

The [Alaska Department of Health (DOH)](https://health.alaska.gov/en/education/rural-health-transformation-program/) administers the program, led by Commissioner **Heidi Hedberg**. DOH has contracted with the **Alaska Community Foundation (ACF)** as its grant administration partner to run the application and award process. Program inquiries go to [RHTP@alaska.gov](mailto:RHTP@alaska.gov); application-status questions to ACF go to RHTP@alaskacf.org.

What is Alaska's rural health transformation plan trying to accomplish?

Alaska is organizing its plan around six RHTP initiative tracks: Healthy Communities, Strengthen Workforce, Healthy Beginnings (maternal health), Spark Technology & Innovation, Health Care Access, and Pay for Value (fiscal sustainability). Recurring themes include retention over recruitment in the workforce, maternal-care access across vast geography (with a Texas-style insurer-partnership sustainability play discussed for maternal health), Indigenous traditional healing, and mobile specialist teams.

[Alaska RHTP hub →](https://health.alaska.gov/en/education/rural-health-transformation-program/)

Where is the money going — which initiatives is Alaska funding, and at what share?

Detailed dollar allocations have not been published as competitive line items yet — Alaska's Letter-of-Interest process is still resolving which of ~1,800 submissions advance to funded awards. Spending is being shaped across the six initiative tracks, with key project tags including **Indigenous Traditional Healing** and **Mobile Specialist Teams**. The full breakdown will follow in Alaska's budget narrative and the state-level RFPs that the LOI round tees up.

What has Alaska procured so far, and what's open right now?

Alaska's first pathway was a **Letter of Interest (LOI)** round: registration opened February 17, 2026 and closed March 11, with ACF receiving **1,797 LOIs from 864 unique organizations** (about 81% Alaska-based). After initial ACF review and an in-depth DOH assessment, outcomes were communicated by email on May 22, 2026, sorting each submission into: advance to full application under the implementation pathway; advance for planning-pathway consideration; deferred (recommended for resubmission); or declined. DOH has cautioned that advancing does not guarantee funding — more applications are advancing than there is money to fund. State-level RFPs are expected to follow. The running record is in the dispatches below.

What are the key upcoming dates and deadlines in Alaska?

Many of the LOI-phase milestones are now past — the LOI window (through March 11, 2026), public comment on the evaluation frameworks (due March 12), and notifications (late May). Alaska has run a heavy calendar of **RHTP Impacts Series** webinars, organized initiative-by-initiative, as its main engagement channel. Upcoming application windows, full-application deadlines and new webinars surface first in the dispatches below and on the DOH RHTP hub and Funding Opportunities & Applicant Information Center.

What legislation or state action enables Alaska's RHTP?

The Rural Health Transformation Program was created by **Section 71401 of the One Big Beautiful Bill Act (2025)** and is administered federally by CMS. Alaska administers its award through the Department of Health, which contracted the Alaska Community Foundation as grant administrator and posted an **RHTP Advisory Council Charter** along with draft Project Evaluation and Portfolio Analysis Review Frameworks for public comment. Detailed allocation figures are set out in Alaska's CMS-approved budget narrative.

How can vendors, providers, or consultants engage with Alaska's program?

Eligibility runs on two tracks. **Direct-to-state contracts** will go out as competitive RFPs where firms bid as **prime contractors**. **Subgrants and subawards** flow to rural providers — hospitals and Tribal health organizations, clinics, EMS and community organizations — and it is here that technology vendors and consultants most often engage as **subcontractors or implementation partners** rather than direct grantees. DOH has explicitly used its Impacts Series webinars as a brokerage layer — framed as a “dating session” where Alaskan providers and outside vendors find each other by initiative track.

Grant administration and applications run through the [Alaska Community Foundation grants portal](https://alaskacf.org/grants/); contribute ideas via the DOH Community Input Form and track updates on the [Alaska RHTP hub](https://health.alaska.gov/en/education/rural-health-transformation-program/).
