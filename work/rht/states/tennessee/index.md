## Questions & answers

What is Tennessee's Rural Health Transformation Program allocation, and how is the funding structured?

Tennessee's Budget Period 1 award is **approximately $206.9 million** — above the $200 million baseline states were asked to apply for — as its share of the federal $50 billion Rural Health Transformation Program, structured by CMS as a five-year program with Year 1 funds obligated by October 31, 2026 and spent by September 30, 2027.

Which agency administers Tennessee's RHTP, and who leads it?

The [Tennessee Department of Health (TDH)](https://www.tn.gov/health/rural) administers the program, running solicitations out of its Division of Strategic Initiatives through the Department of General Services. Deputy Commissioner **JW Randolph** serves as the state's RHTP principal investigator. General program questions go to [RuralHealth@TN.gov](mailto:RuralHealth@TN.gov); the Competitive Procurement Director ([Competitive.Health@tn.gov](mailto:Competitive.Health@tn.gov)) is the sole point of contact for active RFAs.

What is Tennessee's rural health transformation plan trying to accomplish?

Tennessee is building on its existing **Healthcare Resiliency Program (HRP)** model — which has deployed hundreds of millions and supported 110+ projects since 2022 — to expand preventive, behavioral-health, and substance-use care, address maternity deserts and memory care, modernize rural health technology, and extend the safety net into counties with no provider. Its flagship framing is “Make Rural Tennessee Healthy Again” (MaRTHA).

[Tennessee RHTP hub →](https://www.tn.gov/health/rural)

Where is the money going — which initiatives is Tennessee funding, and at what share?

- **Health Technology & Innovation (Long-Range)** — $59,380,009, the single biggest pool.
- **Health Technology & Innovation (Short-Range)** — $33 million.
- **Make Rural Tennessee Healthy Again (MaRTHA)** — $50 million.
- **Memory Care Assessment Network (MCAN)** — a dementia hub-and-spoke network.
- **Maternal & Child Health** — up to 100 awards ($1.25M small / $1.25M–$12.5M large).
- Plus Safety Net Expansion (>$60M), Rural FIMR ($4.5M), HART built-environment grants, and non-emergency medical transportation.

An EMR-replacement cap of 5% and a $225,700 salary cap apply across categories; the full breakdown is in Tennessee's budget narrative.

What has Tennessee procured so far, and what's open right now?

Tennessee is flooding the zone with solicitations. Between July 6 and July 20, 2026 TDH released six RFPs through the Department of General Services — a Statewide eConsult Platform, a CHANT call center, rural non-emergency transportation, a pregnancy/post-partum mobile app, and a Rural Health Innovation Catalyst. Named RFAs include Health Technology & Innovation Short-Range (#34320-20327, due July 27) and Long-Range (#34320-21427, $59.38M, due Aug 3), MaRTHA (#34320-20127, $50M, due Aug 3), and the Memory Care Assessment Network (#34320-19326, due July 20). Notably, for-profit health-tech companies are explicitly eligible as lead applicant. The dispatch list below carries the running record.

What legislation or state action enables Tennessee's RHTP?

The Rural Health Transformation Program was created by **Section 71401 of the One Big Beautiful Bill Act (2025)** and is administered federally by CMS. In Tennessee, the state legislature is separately considering more than $100 million in complementary capital funding, and TennCare is investing $125 million of Shared Savings in targeted rural hospital and primary-care capital projects. Detailed allocation figures are set out in Tennessee's project and budget narratives.

How can vendors, providers, or consultants engage with Tennessee's program?

Eligibility runs on two tracks. **Direct-to-state contracts** go out as competitive RFAs and RFPs, where firms bid as **prime contractors** — and Tennessee has gone unusually far here, confirming repeatedly that **for-profit health-tech companies can be the lead applicant**, not just a subcontractor behind a hospital, with the only hard gate being Tennessee Secretary of State registration before contract execution. Assessments, workflow optimization, and technology roadmaps are fundable as the primary deliverable, opening the door to consulting and advisory firms. **Subgrants and subawards** also flow to rural providers — hospitals, FQHCs, and community-based organizations — where vendors engage as **subcontractors or implementation partners**. Note that awards are cost-reimbursement only, so applicants must be able to float costs.
